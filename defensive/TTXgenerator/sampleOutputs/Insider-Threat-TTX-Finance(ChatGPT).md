# Insider Threat Tabletop Exercise

**Scenario:** Malicious Employee - IP Theft and Backdoor Persistence
**Duration:** 6 Hours (Extended)
**Format:** In-Person
**Audience:** Mixed technical and executive (approx. 15 participants)
**Style:** Traditional (discussion-led, facilitator controlled pacing)

---

## 1. Exercise Overview

### Objectives

* Stress-test **decision-making** under uncertainty and regulatory scrutiny
* Evaluate **crisis management** across Security, Legal, HR, Compliance, and Executive leadership
* Validate insider threat escalation paths and executive governance
* Examine tradeoffs between containment, business continuity, and evidence preservation

### What This Exercise Is

* A facilitated discussion using realistic injects
* A safe environment to explore decisions, not a test of individual performance

### What This Exercise Is Not

* A technical simulation
* A pass or fail assessment

### Participant Roles (Suggested)

* Incident Response Lead
* SOC Manager / Threat Detection
* Insider Threat Program Owner
* Legal Counsel
* Human Resources
* Compliance / Risk
* Executive Sponsor (CISO, CIO, COO, or equivalent)
* Communications or Corporate Affairs (observer or participant)

---

## 2. Facilitation Approach (Traditional Style)

* Facilitator controls inject pacing
* Emphasis on discussion, rationale, and alternatives
* No scoring or artificial time pressure
* Facilitator probes assumptions and decision ownership
* Complications introduced only after discussion concludes

## Scenario Narrative (Participant Preview)

Your organization is a regulated financial services firm handling proprietary trading models, customer financial data, and sensitive risk analytics.

Over the past several weeks, leadership has expressed concern about increased regulatory scrutiny and heightened expectations around data governance, insider risk, and supervisory transparency.

The exercise begins when subtle internal indicators suggest potential misuse of privileged access by a trusted employee. What initially appears to be an isolated anomaly gradually raises broader questions about intent, data exposure, organizational risk, and executive accountability.

Participants must navigate technical uncertainty, employee rights, regulatory obligations, and business impact while maintaining trust and control.

## Key Decision Points and Facilitator Guidance

### Decision Point 1: When Does This Become an Insider Incident?

**Prompt:** What indicators justify escalation from anomaly to insider threat?
**Facilitator Guidance:** Challenge assumptions about trust and seniority.

---

### Decision Point 2: Access Revocation Timing

**Prompt:** Do you immediately revoke access or monitor further?
**Discussion Areas:** Evidence preservation, business risk, employee rights.

---

### Decision Point 3: HR and Legal Engagement

**Prompt:** Who leads coordination and messaging with the employee?
**Facilitator Guidance:** Surface role clarity and authority gaps.

---

### Decision Point 4: Executive Notification

**Prompt:** What does leadership need to know and when?
**Discussion Areas:** Transparency versus incomplete facts.

---

### Decision Point 5: Regulatory Notification Threshold

**Prompt:** When does suspicion become reportable?
**Facilitator Guidance:** Explore conservative versus delayed approaches.

---

### Decision Point 6: Public and Internal Communications

**Prompt:** Do you acknowledge the issue internally or externally?
**Discussion Areas:** Reputation risk, employee morale.
